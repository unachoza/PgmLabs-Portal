**10. Please explain how you will actively engage attendees during your session. Please also describe planned activities.** *[required, 1080 chars]*
> No lecture and no slides required. The session runs as a compressed version of the hackathon itself, in table groups of four to six. (1) Challenge surfacing, 10 minutes: each participant names one real problem from their board, agency, or organization, and the table picks one to work on. (2) Build sprint, 20 minutes: using the AI Innovation Journey worksheet, tables scope an AI-assisted solution to that problem. No coding is required, and non-technical participants are deliberately put in the driver's seat. (3) Feedback round, 10 minutes: tables trade solutions with a neighboring table and stress-test each other's assumptions, mirroring the hackathon's judging round. (4) Partner mapping and debrief, 10 minutes: each table maps who in their own region they would need to convene to actually field this, including employers, community colleges, nonprofits, and technologists, and we compare maps across the room. Short first-person stories from Hack-AI-thon participants are threaded between segments to keep the room grounded in what actually happened rather than theory.
